I work on geometric and structured representation learning for multivariate data, building known structure into models (relationships between variables as graphs or hypergraphs, temporal dynamics through state-space models, covariance features as SPD matrices) instead of leaving the network to rediscover it. My doctoral research uses multichannel EEG as the testbed, where spatial, higher-order, temporal and geometric structure all matter.

Focus Areas

Research Interests

Structure

Geometric & Structured Learning

Learning representations that keep known relational, symmetry and geometric structure instead of treating observations as independent, unordered features. Current work represents covariance features as symmetric positive definite (SPD) matrices under the Log-Euclidean metric, respecting their geometry rather than treating them as unconstrained Euclidean vectors, with persistent-homology-based regularisation.

Graphs

Graph & Hypergraph Learning

Graph and hypergraph neural networks for pairwise, multi-relational and higher-order dependencies in multivariate data, from multi-edge graphs to hypergraphs with distributional and geometric inductive biases. Separate work combines equivariant GNNs that encode bilateral (Z₂) electrode symmetry with continual learning for patient-adaptive detection.

Sequences

State-Space Models

S4 and Mamba state-space models for long-range dependencies in multivariate sequences, integrated with graph and hypergraph representations for joint spatial and temporal modelling. Related work uses Kolmogorov–Arnold Networks to learn feature-wise temporal–spatial gating before classification.

Application

Biomedical Time Series

Multichannel EEG is the main application domain: seizure detection and seizure prediction, using graph, temporal and geometric representations to model relationships within the signals. Longer term, I am interested in models that can run close to real time and adapt to a new patient with limited retraining.
